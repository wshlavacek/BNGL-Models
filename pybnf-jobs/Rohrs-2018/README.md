# Rohrs-2018 — CAR CD3ζ site-specific phosphorylation, parameter fitting (PyBNF jobs)

PyBNF edition-2 parameter-fitting jobs derived from the phosphoproteomic and modeling study of
how a chimeric antigen receptor's CD3ζ signaling domain is phosphorylated by LCK:

> **Rohrs JA, Zheng D, Graham NA, Wang P, Finley SD.** **"Computational model of chimeric antigen
> receptors explains site-specific phosphorylation kinetics."**
> *Biophys J* 2018; **115**(7):1116–1129.
> DOI: [10.1016/j.bpj.2018.08.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpj.2018.08.018) · PMCID: PMC6199440.

This is **Phase 2** of the Rohrs-2018 curation. **Phase 1**
(`models/car_cd3zeta_phosphorylation_rohrs2018/`) captured all four candidate mechanisms, the ITAM
point mutants and the phosphatase prediction as static reference models with their published
parameters. The job here captures the paper's **headline parameter-estimation problem**: recovering
those parameters by fitting the winning mechanism to the measurements.

Built with the `curate-pybnf-job` skill.

## The model

CD3ζ's intracellular domain carries three ITAMs — A, B and C — with two tyrosines each, in the
N- to C-terminal order A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2. His-tagged CD3ζ and constitutively active
LCK-G2A-Y505F were bound to nickel-chelating liposomes at 20,000 and ~20 molecules/µm², ATP was
added, and phosphoproteomic mass spectrometry measured percent phosphorylation at each site over
three hours. The six sites turn over with clearly different half-maximal times (about 15 min at A1
up to 150 min at C1) but with a **Hill coefficient near 1 at every site**.

The mechanism that explains both facts, and that the paper selects on residual error and AIC, is
**competitive inhibition**: LCK works on all six sites in random order with Michaelis-Menten
kinetics, but every tyrosine in the system competes for the kinase through one shared denominator
(Eq. 4) — unphosphorylated sites through K<sub>M,i</sub> and phosphorylated sites through
K<sub>I,i</sub> = K<sub>M,i</sub>·ξ, with a single fitted ξ. Product inhibition is the stronger
effect and is what makes the response flatten late without steepening any individual site.

The network is finite and tiny (65 species, 192 reactions), so the model is cheap; the fit is a
7-parameter search over nine conditions.

Mutants are **Conditions, not separate models**: each ITAM tyrosine carries a 0/1 gate parameter
`live_<site>`, and setting it to zero removes that site from both the substrate and the inhibitor
sums of Eq. 4 — exactly what a tyrosine-to-phenylalanine substitution does. All seven constructs
therefore share one 65-species network, and each mutant is a one-row PEtab `conditions.tsv` entry.

The objective is plain **sum of squares**, which is the sum of squared error the paper itself scores
with (Materials and Methods, *Comparison of model structures*), so the job stays inside the
**PEtab-exportable subset**. The exported PEtab v2 problem is committed as a generated byproduct
under [`cd3zeta_competitive_inhibition/petab/`](cd3zeta_competitive_inhibition/petab/) (regenerate
with `make_petab.py`) — the first PEtab rendering of this problem, since the authors distribute
their models as BioNetGen files (Data S1–S5) and report the fit only in figures.
